Based on the comments and structural patterns found in the discussion on r/hardware, here are three candidate distinctions to be used for the project:

## Deep Technical/Roadmap Argument vs. High-Level Anecdote

Pattern: Some comments provide concrete engineering specifications, process node terminology (such as GAA-FETs, CFETs, or IRDS roadmaps), and links or references to industry roadmaps. In contrast, other comments rely entirely on broad consumer generalizations or personal anecdotes about older devices (like how long a phone lasts or whether an old laptop still runs web tasks).

Candidate Distinction: Posts that cite specific industry roadmaps, transistor physics, or architectural changes vs. posts that speak in general terms about consumer experiences or tech frustration.

## Hardware-Bound Bottlenecks vs. Software/Developer Inefficiency

Pattern: One distinct structural group of comments argues that physical and material limits (like quantum tunneling, memory bandwidth limits, or the slowdown of Moore's Law) dictate future performance walls. Another prominent group blames software bloat, arguing that modern applications and game engines fail to utilize existing hardware features (such as mesh shading or proper memory management).

Candidate Distinction: Comments attributing future performance issues strictly to physical manufacturing walls versus comments arguing that poor software optimization and bloated code are the true bottlenecks.

## Nuanced Industry Critique vs. Snarky Dismissal

Pattern: Several users break down multi-part structural arguments complete with data points (e.g., die sizes, bus widths, power targets, and cost scaling tradeoffs). Meanwhile, others offer short, dismissive quips or emotional rants about corporate greed or "AI slop" without technical backing.

Candidate Distinction: Constructive technical analysis containing numerical or architectural comparisons versus brief emotional reactions or internet sarcasm.###
